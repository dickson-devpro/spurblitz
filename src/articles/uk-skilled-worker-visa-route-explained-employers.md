**If you want to hire a worker from outside the UK, the Skilled Worker visa route is almost certainly the mechanism you will use — and as the sponsoring employer, the compliance burden sits squarely with you. Understanding exactly how the route works, what it costs, and where HR teams routinely go wrong is not optional; it is the foundation of a legally sound international hiring strategy.**

## Why This Matters in 2026

The post-Brexit immigration landscape has now had several years to bed in, but 2026 brings a cluster of pressures that make employer literacy around the Skilled Worker route more urgent than ever. Salary thresholds were significantly uplifted in 2024 and remain at elevated levels, making it more expensive to sponsor lower-paid roles. UK labour shortages persist in technology, engineering, healthcare, and professional services — sectors where overseas talent pipelines are often the only realistic solution. Meanwhile, the Home Office has intensified compliance visits and enforcement action against sponsors who fail to meet their reporting obligations.

For international businesses establishing a UK presence — or US companies that operate transatlantic teams — understanding the UK Skilled Worker route is increasingly a baseline operational skill, sitting alongside knowledge of how to structure employment contracts and manage cross-border payroll. ## How the UK Points-Based System Works

The Skilled Worker route operates on a points-based model. An applicant must accumulate a minimum of 70 points, broken into mandatory and tradeable categories. As an employer, your primary job is to ensure that the job offer you make satisfies the mandatory requirements before any visa application is lodged.

### The 70-Point Breakdown

| Points | Criterion | Mandatory or Tradeable |
|--------|-----------|------------------------|
| 20 | Valid job offer from a licensed sponsor | Mandatory |
| 20 | Role is at or above RQF Level 3 (A-level equivalent) | Mandatory |
| 10 | English language requirement met | Mandatory |
| 20 | Salary meets general threshold **or** going rate | Mandatory (or tradeable) |
| 0–20 | Shortage occupation, PhD relevant to role, or new entrant status | Tradeable (can offset salary) |

The practical implication: if your offered salary is slightly below the full going rate, a shortage occupation designation or the worker's PhD qualification can make up the deficit. But neither you nor the worker can trade away the absolute salary floor for the role.

## Step 1: Become a Licensed Sponsor

Nothing else in this process is possible until your organisation holds a valid Skilled Worker sponsor licence. The Home Office issues licences to UK-based employers who can demonstrate a genuine operating presence, suitable HR infrastructure, and a legitimate need to recruit internationally.

If you have not yet applied, the [How to Apply for a UK Skilled Worker Sponsor Licence](/how-to-apply-uk-skilled-worker-sponsor-licence/) guide walks through the full application in detail. For a more condensed, step-by-step HR workflow, see the [Sponsor Licence Application Step by Step for HR 2026](/sponsor-licence-application-step-by-step-hr/) resource. Gathering the right paperwork upfront is critical — the [Sponsor Licence Application Documents Checklist 2026](/sponsor-licence-application-documents-checklist/) sets out exactly what you will need.

Standard processing takes around eight weeks. A fee-based priority service is available and can reduce this to around ten working days, though availability is not guaranteed. For a realistic timeline analysis, read [How Long Does a Sponsor Licence Application Take? 2026](/how-long-does-sponsor-licence-application-take/).

**Key licence facts for 2026:**

- **Small/charitable sponsors:** £536 application fee
- **Medium/large sponsors:** £1,476 application fee
- Licences are valid for four years and must be renewed
- A licence is rated either A-rated (compliant) or B-rated (improvement plan required)

## Step 2: Identify the Correct Occupation Code

Every role you sponsor must be mapped to a Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code. The Home Office publishes an Appendix Skilled Occupations list that specifies which SOC codes are eligible for the Skilled Worker route, what the going rate is for each, and whether any shortage occupation discount applies.

Choosing the wrong SOC code — even inadvertently — is one of the most common and damaging errors sponsors make. It can result in the visa application being refused or, worse, expose your organisation to a compliance audit.

**Practical tip:** Do not map roles based on job title alone. Read the SOC code description carefully and check whether the actual duties align. A "software engineer" might fall under several different codes depending on whether the role is primarily development, architecture, or data-focused. If you are building an international engineering team, our [Recruiting Software Engineers Internationally (2026 Guide)](/recruiting-software-engineers-internationally/) discusses how to structure roles and job descriptions for compliance purposes.

## Step 3: Understand the Salary Requirements in Full

The salary rules are the area where most employer mistakes cluster. There are two figures you must always check:

1. **The general threshold** — £38,700 per year in 2026 (for most roles)
2. **The going rate for the specific SOC code** — published in the Appendix Skilled Occupations

You must pay **whichever is higher**.

### Salary Discounts: When Do They Apply?

| Worker Category | Discount Available | Floor |
|---|---|---|
| New entrant (under 26, in postdoc training, or switching from student visa) | 20% off going rate | Must still meet general threshold × 0.8 |
| Shortage occupation | 20% off going rate | Must still meet general threshold × 0.8 |
| PhD relevant to the role | 10% off going rate | Must still meet general threshold × 0.9 |
| Standard (no qualifying factor) | None | Full going rate or £38,700, whichever higher |

### Illustrative Worked Example: Software Developer Role

*This is an illustrative example using rounded figures for clarity. Actual going rates change; always verify against the current Appendix Skilled Occupations before issuing a CoS.*

> - **Role:** Software Developer, SOC 2136
> - **Going rate for SOC 2136:** £45,000 per year (illustrative)
> - **General threshold:** £38,700
> - **Candidate:** New entrant (qualifying for 20% discount)
> - **Discounted going rate:** £45,000 × 0.80 = £36,000
> - **General threshold × 0.80:** £38,700 × 0.80 = £30,960
> - **Minimum salary employer must offer:** £36,000 (the discounted going rate, which exceeds the discounted general threshold)

> - **Same role, standard candidate (no discount):**
> - Minimum salary: £45,000 (the going rate, which exceeds £38,700)

The difference between these two scenarios is £9,000 per year in payroll cost, before employer National Insurance — a material figure in headcount budgeting.

## Step 4: Issue a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)

Once you have identified the candidate and confirmed the role qualifies, you assign a Certificate of Sponsorship through the Sponsor Management System (SMS). There are two types:

- **Defined CoS:** Used when the worker is applying from outside the UK or has been allocated a specific certificate in advance. Requires Home Office allocation for roles above the occupation code going rate threshold in some cases.
- **Undefined CoS:** Used for workers already in the UK switching visa categories. You can hold a bank of these and assign them as needed.

**CoS fees in 2026:**

- Small sponsors: £239 per CoS
- Medium/large sponsors: £525 per CoS

The CoS contains key details about the role: job title, SOC code, salary, start date, and duration. Errors on the CoS — even minor ones like an incorrect start date — can lead to visa refusals. The worker cannot correct CoS data; if it is wrong, you must withdraw it and issue a new one, losing the fee.

## Step 5: Pay the Immigration Skills Charge

The Immigration Skills Charge (ISC) is an often-overlooked but significant cost that most sponsors must pay at CoS assignment. It covers the full duration of the visa being requested.

**ISC rates in 2026:**

| Sponsor Size | Per Year of Visa | 5-Year Total |
|---|---|---|
| Small / charitable | £364 | £1,820 |
| Medium / large | £1,000 | £5,000 |

There are exemptions — notably for PhD-level roles in specific SOC codes and for some student-to-worker switches — but these are narrow. ## Step 6: Support the Worker's Visa Application

Once the CoS is assigned, the worker uses the reference number to apply for their Skilled Worker visa through the UK Visas and Immigration online system. As the employer, you do not submit the visa application — but your support matters in practical terms.

The worker will need to pay:
- The visa application fee (currently around £719–£1,420 depending on duration)
- The Immigration Health Surcharge (£1,035 per year in 2026, though this figure is subject to parliamentary change)

Some employers fund these costs as part of their relocation package. If you do, your employment contract should specify whether these amounts are recoverable if the employee leaves within a defined period — and your contract terms need to be drafted carefully. ## Step 7: Ongoing Sponsor Compliance

Holding a sponsor licence is not a one-time administrative act — it is a live regulatory relationship with the Home Office. Sponsors are expected to:

- Conduct right-to-work checks before employment begins and at each visa renewal
- Report certain events within 10 working days (e.g., worker does not show up for first day, worker's role changes materially)
- Report other events within 20 working days (e.g., worker's absences, salary changes)
- Keep records of contact details, payslips, and attendance for every sponsored worker
- Cooperate with unannounced compliance visits

Failure at any point in the compliance cycle risks the Home Office downgrading your licence to B-rated — which means you cannot sponsor new workers until remedial action is complete — or outright revocation. For a detailed guide to your ongoing obligations including licence renewal, see [Sponsor Licence Renewal & Reporting Changes: 2026 Guide](/sponsor-licence-renewal-reporting-changes/).

## Common Employer Mistakes on the Skilled Worker Route — and How to Avoid Them

1. **Applying for a sponsor licence without adequate HR systems in place.**
   *Solution:* Before applying, document your right-to-work check process, your absence monitoring procedure, and your record-keeping framework. The Home Office assesses whether your internal systems are fit for purpose, not just whether you have filled in the form correctly. Organisations that apply without this groundwork are significantly more likely to be refused. Read [Why Sponsor Licence Applications Get Refused (2026)](/why-sponsor-licence-applications-get-refused/) before you submit.

2. **Picking the wrong SOC code for the role.**
   *Solution:* Cross-reference the actual duties in your job description against the SOC code description in the Immigration: Skilled Occupations appendix. If there is any doubt, take legal advice before assigning a CoS.

3. **Offering a salary that meets the general threshold but not the going rate.**
   *Solution:* Always check both the £38,700 floor and the SOC-specific going rate. Pay whichever is higher. Budget for the full going rate before you advertise the role.

4. **Forgetting to include the ISC in the cost model.**
   *Solution:* Model the total cost of a sponsored hire — including the licence fee (amortised), CoS fee, ISC, and visa costs — before signing off on headcount approvals. A medium/large sponsor hiring a software developer for five years faces an ISC liability of £5,000 alone.

5. **Issuing a CoS with incorrect details.**
   *Solution:* Build a pre-issue checklist: job title, SOC code, annual salary (gross), role start date, and visa end date all require careful verification. Have a second reviewer check every CoS before it is assigned.

6. **Not conducting a right-to-work check on day one.**
   *Solution:* Right-to-work checks are a legal obligation, not an HR nicety. For sponsored workers, check the visa conditions (including work restrictions) on the day employment begins and record the check in your files. A digital share code check via the Home Office online system is the standard method in 2026.

7. **Failing to report a sponsored worker leaving the business.**
   *Solution:* If a sponsored worker resigns, is made redundant, or is dismissed, you must report this through the SMS within 10 working days. Missing this deadline is a reportable breach that goes on your compliance record.

8. **Assuming the route is only for large corporates.**
   *Solution:* The Skilled Worker route is available to businesses of any size. Smaller employers should read the [Small Business Guide to Getting a Sponsor Licence 2026](/small-business-guide-getting-sponsor-licence/) and explore whether their IT and HR infrastructure meets the minimum threshold before applying.

## Building a Repeatable International Hiring Process

The employers who manage the Skilled Worker route most effectively treat it as a repeatable system, not a one-off project. That means:

- Maintaining a pre-vetted list of occupation codes relevant to your hiring patterns
- Building CoS issuance into a standardised HR workflow with clear sign-off steps
- Training your HR team annually on changes to salary thresholds and shortage occupation lists
- Keeping the SMS up to date proactively — not reactively

Once you understand [what a sponsor licence lets you do once approved](/what-sponsor-licence-lets-you-do-once-approved/), it becomes clear that the licence is a genuine strategic asset. It enables you to access a global talent pool when domestic hiring falls short, to build international teams, and to offer visa sponsorship as a meaningful benefit in competitive recruitment.
