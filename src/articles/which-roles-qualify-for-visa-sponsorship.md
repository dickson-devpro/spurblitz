**Not every vacancy can be sponsored — eligibility for visa sponsorship depends on a specific combination of occupation type, required skill level, and minimum salary that each destination country's immigration system defines. Understanding exactly which roles clear those thresholds is the single most important step employers and internationally mobile workers can take before committing resources to a cross-border hire.** Here, we break down the qualifying criteria for the two most active English-speaking sponsorship markets — the United States and the United Kingdom — and show you how to assess any specific role before a job offer is made.

---

## Why This Matters in 2026

The global competition for skilled workers has not softened. If anything, the regulatory environment around visa sponsorship has become more precisely calibrated in 2026: both the US and UK have adjusted salary thresholds upward, tightened occupation code lists, and increased compliance auditing of sponsoring employers. For businesses recruiting internationally, the cost of misjudging role eligibility is significant — wasted legal fees, failed visa applications, and a candidate left in limbo.

For internationally mobile workers, the stakes are equally high. Accepting a job offer under the assumption it will be sponsored, only to discover the role does not meet occupation or salary criteria, can affect immigration status, financial planning, and career timelines simultaneously. With some [staff hiring costs when recruiting from overseas](/staff-hiring-costs-recruiting-from-overseas/) running to tens of thousands of dollars once legal fees, relocation, and onboarding are factored in, getting the eligibility question right upfront is not a formality — it is the foundation of the entire process.

---

## The Core Logic Behind Sponsorship Eligibility

Every country that operates an employer-sponsored visa system is trying to answer the same question: is this role something that genuinely requires an overseas worker because the domestic labour market cannot adequately supply it at a fair wage? That logic produces three eligibility levers that appear in almost every system:

1. **Occupation classification** — the role must fall within an approved or eligible category of work.
2. **Skill or qualification level** — the duties must require a defined minimum level of education or demonstrable expertise.
3. **Salary floor** — the wage offered must meet or exceed a prescribed minimum, protecting domestic workers from wage undercutting.

All three must be satisfied simultaneously. A high salary cannot compensate for an ineligible occupation code, and a prestigious job title means nothing if the actual duties are primarily unskilled.

## Which Roles Qualify in the United States

### H-1B: Specialty Occupation

The H-1B is the most widely used US employment visa category for foreign nationals with a bachelor's degree or higher. To qualify, USCIS requires that the role meets the definition of a **specialty occupation**, meaning it requires:

- Theoretical and practical application of a body of highly specialised knowledge, **and**
- At minimum, a US bachelor's degree (or its equivalent) in a specific field directly related to the position.

The key phrase is "specific field." A software engineering role requiring a computer science degree is a textbook specialty occupation. A general "manager" role where any bachelor's degree field is acceptable is far more likely to face scrutiny or denial.

**Roles that routinely qualify for H-1B sponsorship:**

| Occupation Category | Examples | Typical SOC Code Range |
|---|---|---|
| Software & IT | Software engineers, data scientists, cybersecurity analysts | 15-1200s |
| Architecture & Engineering | Civil, mechanical, electrical engineers | 17-1000s / 17-2000s |
| Healthcare (clinical) | Physicians, surgeons, dentists (via J or O routes too) | 29-1000s |
| Life & Physical Sciences | Biochemists, chemists, physicists | 19-1000s / 19-2000s |
| Business & Finance (specialist) | Financial analysts, actuaries, management analysts | 13-2000s |
| Education | University faculty, specialised researchers | 25-1000s |
| Architecture & Design | Architects, urban planners | 17-1011 / 19-3051 |

Roles in general sales, administrative support, manufacturing labour, or hospitality typically do not qualify for H-1B sponsorship because they do not require a specific degree in a directly related field.

### H-1B Prevailing Wage

The Department of Labor (DOL) assigns prevailing wages to each SOC code by geography at four experience levels. The employer must pay the **higher** of the actual wage paid to comparable workers or the prevailing wage. In practice, prevailing wages for Level II–III positions in major metro areas frequently exceed $100,000. An employer offering below the prevailing wage for a given SOC code and location will be refused.

For a detailed breakdown of H-1B and related nonimmigrant categories, including H-2B for temporary non-agricultural roles and H-2A for agricultural workers, see our companion piece [H Visa Types Explained for US Employers (2026 Guide)](/h-visa-types-explained-us-employers/).

### EB Immigrant Visas (Green Card Routes)

For permanent sponsorship, US employers most commonly use:

- **EB-2**: Advanced degree professionals or those with exceptional ability
- **EB-3**: Skilled workers (minimum 2 years training/experience), professionals (bachelor's), and unskilled workers
- **EB-1**: Priority workers — extraordinary ability, outstanding professors and researchers, multinational managers

EB-1A and EB-1B are attractive because they do not require PERM labor certification (the government's labor market test). All other EB categories except EB-1C typically do. PERM is not a visa; it is a prerequisite that proves no qualified US workers were available at the time of hire.

### L-1 Intracompany Transferees

Multinational companies can transfer employees in **managerial, executive, or specialised knowledge** roles via the L-1 visa. This does not require an SOC code match in the same way H-1B does, but the role must genuinely be managerial, executive, or involve specialised proprietary knowledge. L-1 is not a route for entry-level hires.

### O-1: Extraordinary Ability

O-1 visas exist for individuals with extraordinary ability in sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics (O-1A) or extraordinary achievement in film/TV (O-1B). This is not an employer-controlled classification in the same way — the focus is on the individual's documented achievements rather than the job's SOC code. It requires sustained national or international acclaim supported by extensive evidence.

## Which Roles Qualify in the United Kingdom

The UK's primary employment route is the **Skilled Worker visa**, which replaced Tier 2 (General) post-Brexit and continues to be administered through the points-based system. For an employer to sponsor a worker on this route, the role must:

1. **Be on the eligible occupation list** (published by the Home Office and linked to Standard Occupational Classification codes).
2. **Be at RQF Level 3 or above** (roughly A-level equivalent or higher) — though the vast majority of roles on the list sit at RQF 6 (degree level).
3. **Meet the salary threshold** — currently £38,700 per year or the "going rate" for the occupation code, whichever is higher.

For employers who have not yet obtained a sponsor licence, the process is a prerequisite to sponsoring anyone. Our guide on [how to apply for a UK Skilled Worker Sponsor Licence](/how-to-apply-uk-skilled-worker-sponsor-licence/) covers the application mechanics, while the [Sponsor Licence Application Documents Checklist 2026](/sponsor-licence-application-documents-checklist/) helps you gather the paperwork before you begin.

### UK Occupation Code Categories That Commonly Qualify

| SOC 2020 Group | Examples | Typical Salary Going Rate Range |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Managers and Directors | IT directors, finance directors, operations managers | £40,000–£80,000+ |
| 2 — Professional Occupations | Engineers, doctors, teachers, lawyers, scientists | £30,000–£70,000+ |
| 3 — Associate Professional | Technicians, IT specialists, nurses | £28,000–£50,000 |
| 4 — Administrative (select) | Some specialist admin roles (very limited) | Varies |

Note that group 4 and below rarely appears on the eligible occupation list at the required skill level. Health and care roles have their own specific qualifying conditions, including registration with the relevant professional body (GMC, NMC, etc.).

### The Immigration Salary List (ISL)

The ISL (which replaced the Shortage Occupation List) identifies specific roles where the salary threshold may be reduced by up to 20% below the general £38,700 floor. This is particularly relevant in sectors such as nursing, certain engineering disciplines, and some IT roles. However, the ISL is reviewed periodically, and employers should verify current codes with UKVI rather than relying on historical lists.

### Intra-Company Transfer Route (UK Global Business Mobility)

Similar to the US L-1, the UK's Senior or Specialist Worker route (part of Global Business Mobility) allows multinationals to transfer staff in senior or specialist roles. The salary floor is higher (£48,500 for most applicants or the going rate), and there is no public immigration salary list discount available.

## Comparing US and UK Qualifying Criteria Side by Side

| Criterion | United States (H-1B) | United Kingdom (Skilled Worker) |
|---|---|---|
| Occupation eligibility | Specialty occupation (degree-specific) | Eligible SOC code at RQF 3+ |
| Minimum qualification | Bachelor's in directly related field | RQF 3 minimum; RQF 6 most common |
| Salary floor | DOL prevailing wage by SOC/location | £38,700 or going rate (whichever higher) |
| Labour market test | Not required for H-1B (PERM for EB) | Not required (but sponsor must prove genuine vacancy) |
| Employer prerequisite | Must be a registered US employer; H-1B cap applies | Must hold active sponsor licence |
| Temporary or permanent | Temporary (up to 6 years initially) | Temporary (up to 5 years; settlement eligible) |
| Annual cap | Yes (H-1B regular cap + advanced degree exemption) | No cap |

## Worked Illustrative Examples

*The following examples are illustrative only and use fictionalised figures. They do not represent specific cases or guarantee outcomes.*

### Example 1: Software Engineer — UK Skilled Worker

**Scenario:** A London-based fintech startup wants to sponsor a backend software engineer from Brazil. The role requires Python expertise and 3+ years of commercial experience.

- **SOC Code check:** The role maps to SOC 2132 (IT business analysts, architects, and systems designers) or 2135 (IT engineers). Both are on the eligible occupation list at RQF 6.
- **Salary check:** The going rate for SOC 2135 in inner London is approximately £45,000. The general threshold is £38,700. The employer must offer at least £45,000.
- **Outcome (illustrative):** If the employer offers £47,500 and holds a valid sponsor licence, the role qualifies. At £36,000, it does not — regardless of the candidate's quality.

For employers building out international tech teams, [recruiting software engineers internationally](/recruiting-software-engineers-internationally/) covers sourcing strategy alongside these compliance foundations.

### Example 2: General Sales Manager — US H-1B

**Scenario:** A retail chain wants to sponsor a Canadian national as a regional sales manager. The candidate holds an MBA.

- **Specialty occupation test:** Sales management typically does not require a degree in a specific field. USCIS has denied H-1B petitions for generic sales and management roles where any business degree suffices.
- **Possible alternative:** If the role involves specialised financial analysis, operations research, or a highly industry-specific technical function, it may be reframed — but the actual duties must genuinely match. Retrofitting a job description is a compliance risk, not a solution.
- **Outcome (illustrative):** As described, this role is unlikely to qualify for H-1B. The employer might explore L-1A (if this is a multinational transfer of a manager) or TN status (available to Canadian and Mexican nationals in designated NAFTA/USMCA occupations).

### Example 3: Nurse — UK Skilled Worker (ISL Role)

**Scenario:** An NHS trust wants to sponsor a registered nurse from the Philippines.

- **SOC Code:** 2231 (Nurses) is on the eligible occupation list and has historically appeared on the ISL.
- **Salary:** If the role qualifies under the ISL, the threshold may be lower than the standard £38,700 — but the employer must verify current ISL status and the specific going rate.
- **Professional registration:** The candidate must hold NMC registration (or be in the process of obtaining it) before the Certificate of Sponsorship can be assigned.
- **Outcome (illustrative):** If NMC registration is in place, salary meets the applicable rate, and the employer holds a sponsor licence covering health and care roles, this role qualifies.

## Common Mistakes Employers Make When Assessing Role Eligibility

1. **Relying on the job title rather than the job duties.**
   *Solution:* Map actual daily responsibilities to the relevant SOC or occupation code. Titles like "Coordinator," "Associate," or "Specialist" can describe both eligible and ineligible roles depending on what the work actually involves.

2. **Assuming any degree-requiring role qualifies for H-1B.**
   *Solution:* The degree must be in a directly and specifically related field. A role requiring "any bachelor's degree" almost certainly fails the specialty occupation test. Work with an immigration attorney to audit the job description before filing.

3. **Checking the salary threshold without checking the going rate.**
   *Solution:* In the UK, the going rate for specific occupation codes frequently exceeds the general floor. Checking only the £38,700 headline figure without looking up the SOC-specific going rate is a common and costly error.

4. **Failing to confirm that the employer is eligible to sponsor at all.**
   *Solution:* Before assessing any individual role, confirm the business holds — or can obtain — a sponsor licence (UK) or is a legitimate registered US employer capable of filing petitions. Role eligibility is moot if the employer cannot legally sponsor.

5. **Overlooking the timing of professional registration requirements.**
   *Solution:* For regulated professions (medicine, nursing, law, engineering in some jurisdictions), professional body registration is a condition of sponsorship, not something that can follow later. Build registration timelines into your hiring plan. See [how to onboard an employee relocating from abroad](/how-to-onboard-employee-relocating-from-abroad/) for practical pre-arrival sequencing.

6. **Not accounting for location-based wage variation in the US.**
   *Solution:* DOL prevailing wages differ significantly between, for example, rural Alabama and San Francisco. A wage that satisfies the prevailing rate in one metro may fall short in another. If the worker may be placed at multiple sites, seek legal advice on which wage level applies.

7. **Assuming the same role qualifies the same way in every country.**
   *Solution:* If you are building a cross-border team across multiple markets, assess each country's rules independently. Germany, for example, operates its own skilled migration framework — our [Germany Work Visa Routes for Employers: 2026 Guide](/germany-work-visa-routes-employers/) covers the Fachkräfteeinwanderungsgesetz and what qualifies there.

## How to Check Role Eligibility: A Practical Framework

Before posting any role as "visa sponsorship available," work through this sequence:

1. **Draft a precise job description** — actual duties, minimum qualifications, and required experience. Avoid inflating requirements and avoid vague scope.
2. **Identify the occupation code** — US: SOC code via the Bureau of Labor Statistics. UK: SOC 2020 code via the ONS or Home Office eligible occupations list.
3. **Verify skill level** — Does the role fall within the required RQF band (UK) or specialty occupation definition (US)?
4. **Check the salary** — US: run the DOL wage library for the SOC code and the relevant metro area. UK: check the going rate in the Home Office guidance for the relevant SOC code and compare to the general threshold.
5. **Confirm your sponsorship infrastructure** — Do you hold a sponsor licence (UK) or have existing immigration counsel and filing capability (US)?
6. **Flag regulated profession requirements** — Is professional body registration a prerequisite?
7. **Document your assessment** — If an application is challenged, a well-documented role eligibility assessment supports your case.

When you are ready to move from assessment to active hiring, [screening international job candidates fairly](/screening-international-job-candidates-fairly/) and [writing job postings that attract overseas applicants](/writing-job-postings-attract-overseas-applicants/) will help you build a compliant and effective pipeline.

## What Disqualifies a Role

Some characteristics push roles outside sponsorship eligibility regardless of salary or candidate quality:

- **Primarily self-directed with no bona fide employer-employee relationship** (disqualifies H-1B in particular)
- **Below the required skill or qualification threshold** (disqualifies UK Skilled Worker for roles below RQF 3)
- **Not on the eligible occupation list** (UK specifically)
- **General management roles without specialised knowledge in a defined field** (problematic for H-1B and L-1B)
- **Roles that are primarily commission-based with no guaranteed base salary** meeting the threshold
- **Positions that exist primarily to fulfil a short-term operational need** that would ordinarily be filled by a contractor rather than a direct employee

## Building a Sustainable Sponsorship Programme

Qualifying the role is the first step, not the last. Employers who run international hiring programmes at scale need to maintain sponsor compliance, manage certificate of sponsorship (CoS) allocations in the UK, track prevailing wage obligations, and stay current as thresholds and occupation lists are updated annually.
