**A UK sponsor licence approval is the moment your business gains the legal authority to hire skilled workers from anywhere in the world. From that point, you can issue Certificates of Sponsorship, access the Sponsorship Management System, and build an international workforce — but the licence also binds you to a detailed set of ongoing compliance obligations that carry real consequences if ignored.**

## What Approval Actually Means in Practice

Many employers treat licence approval as a finish line. In reality, it is a starting line. The moment UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) grants your licence, you are handed a set of powers that most businesses cannot access — along with a corresponding set of duties that you are now legally accountable for.

Understanding both sides of this equation is essential. The powers let you compete globally for talent. The duties, if neglected, can strip those powers away. This guide walks through every significant capability and obligation that a sponsor licence unlocks, with worked examples to make the mechanics concrete.

## Why This Matters in 2026

The post-Brexit immigration landscape has matured significantly, but 2026 brings its own pressures. Salary thresholds for the Skilled Worker route were raised in 2024 and remain elevated, meaning the cost of sponsoring a worker is higher than in previous years. At the same time, domestic skill shortages in sectors including technology, healthcare, engineering, and finance mean that many employers — including foreign-owned businesses operating in the UK — cannot find sufficient candidates from the domestic labour market alone.

For foreign nationals running UK subsidiaries, branch offices, or newly incorporated companies, a sponsor licence is often the mechanism that makes a UK operation viable at all. Founders who relocated to the UK and obtained their own visa through other means still need a licence to bring international colleagues, regional managers, or specialist engineers into the business. The licence is, in short, one of the most commercially significant immigration assets a UK employer can hold.

## Permission 1: Assigning Certificates of Sponsorship

The core commercial power a sponsor licence unlocks is the ability to assign a **Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)**. Despite the name, a CoS is not a physical certificate — it is a unique alphanumeric reference code generated in the Sponsorship Management System and assigned to a specific worker for a specific role.

The CoS contains key information about the job: the Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code, the salary, the start date, and the employer's details. The overseas worker uses this reference number as a central component of their visa application.

### Defined vs. Undefined CoS

There are two types of CoS, and understanding the difference matters operationally:

| Type | When Used | Who Approves | Process |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defined CoS** | Workers applying from outside the UK | UKVI — you must request each one | Requested via SMS; UKVI typically responds within one working day |
| **Undefined CoS** | Workers already in the UK switching visa routes | Allocated to you in a bank | Drawn from your annual allocation; assigned in SMS without separate UKVI approval |

**Illustrative Example:** GlobalTech GmbH opens a UK subsidiary and hires a UK-based immigration solicitor to handle its sponsor licence application. The licence is approved in April 2026. GlobalTech wants to bring over its Berlin-based lead software engineer, Katarina, who will apply from Germany. GlobalTech must request a Defined CoS from UKVI via the SMS, specifying the role details. UKVI approves it, GlobalTech assigns it to Katarina, and she includes the reference number in her Skilled Worker visa application at the British consulate in Berlin.

Meanwhile, GlobalTech also wants to promote Rajan, a current employee already in the UK on a Graduate visa, into a qualifying Skilled Worker role. For Rajan, GlobalTech draws an Undefined CoS from its existing allocation and assigns it without needing separate UKVI approval.

## Permission 2: Access to the Sponsorship Management System (SMS)

Within a few working days of licence approval, your designated **Level 1 User** receives login credentials for the Sponsorship Management System — UKVI's online portal for all sponsor-related activity.

The SMS is where you:

- Assign and manage Certificates of Sponsorship
- Report changes and events relating to sponsored workers
- Update your organisation's details (address, HR contacts, licence information)
- Add or remove Key Personnel (Authorising Officer, Key Contact, Level 1 and Level 2 Users)
- Request additional CoS allocations

Think of the SMS as your compliance control centre. Familiarity with it is not optional — most of your ongoing statutory duties are discharged through this system, and missing an SMS action within the required timeframe can trigger a compliance visit or worse.

## Permission 3: Sponsoring Workers Across Multiple Eligible Routes

A single sponsor licence can cover multiple immigration routes, though the routes must be specified when you apply. The most common routes for commercial employers in 2026 are:

| Immigration Route | Primary Use Case | Licence Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Skilled Worker** | Permanent or long-term hires in eligible occupations | Worker licence |
| **Senior or Specialist Worker (GSM)** | Intra-company transfers of senior staff or specialists | Worker licence |
| **Graduate Trainee (GSM)** | Transferring graduate trainees for structured programmes | Worker licence |
| **Scale-up Worker** | Fast-growing businesses sponsoring high-skilled workers | Worker licence |
| **Temporary Worker routes** | Short-term or seasonal placements | Temporary Worker licence |

Foreign-owned businesses with operations in multiple countries frequently use the Global Business Mobility routes to move senior employees between offices without those employees needing to go through the standard Skilled Worker recruitment process. This is a significant operational advantage — the Senior or Specialist Worker route does not require a Resident Labour Market Test and has its own salary rules.

## Permission 4: Building a Compliant HR Infrastructure

Approval implicitly grants you something beyond legal permissions — it grants your business legitimacy in the eyes of UKVI as a trusted employer. But that trust is conditional on maintaining the systems that justified it.

Once licenced, you are expected to have — and actively use — the following:

**Right-to-work check systems.** You must conduct right-to-work checks on every employee before employment begins, not just sponsored workers. For sponsored workers, checks must be repeated at visa expiry.

**Absence tracking.** You must monitor and record sponsored workers' absences, particularly any unauthorised absence exceeding 10 consecutive working days, which must be reported to UKVI.

**Salary and role monitoring.** If you promote a sponsored worker, reduce their hours, or materially change their role, you may need to assign a new CoS or at minimum report the change.

**Contact and address records.** UKVI must always be able to contact your Authorising Officer. If that person changes, you must update the SMS promptly.

## Permission 5: Renewing and Growing Your Licence

A sponsor licence lasts four years. Before expiry, you must apply for renewal to continue sponsoring workers. Crucially, your ability to retain a licence in good standing over those four years — and to renew it — depends entirely on your compliance record.

A clean record can also support requests for increased CoS allocations, which is practically important for growing businesses. If your initial allocation of, say, 10 Undefined CoS runs out because your business expanded faster than anticipated, you can submit an online request for more. UKVI reviews these requests and may ask you to justify the additional need.

## Compliance Obligations: The Other Side of Approval

Every power listed above comes paired with an obligation. The table below maps the key ongoing duties to their timeframes:

| Duty | Timeframe | Consequence of Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Report worker does not arrive | 10 working days from expected start date | Licence downgrade or revocation |
| Report unauthorised absence | 10 working days from the 10th day of absence | Licence downgrade |
| Report worker resignation or dismissal | 10 working days | Licence downgrade |
| Report significant changes to worker's role | As soon as reasonably practicable | Civil penalty, licence action |
| Report change of Authorising Officer | Promptly via SMS | Compliance risk |
| Conduct repeat right-to-work check | Before visa expiry | Illegal working penalty |
| Renew licence | Before 4-year expiry date | Licence lapses; CoS becomes invalid |

These are not theoretical risks. UKVI conducts both pre-licence and post-licence compliance visits — sometimes announced, sometimes unannounced. Inspectors may review your HR records, interview Key Personnel, and audit your CoS assignments. Businesses that fail these visits can have their licence downgraded from A-rating to B-rating (restricting CoS assignment) or revoked entirely.

## Illustrative Worked Example: The First 90 Days After Approval

**Scenario:** Miriam runs a London-based fintech startup. She originally arrived in the UK on an Innovator Founder visa and recently secured a sponsor licence for her now-incorporated company. Her company has five UK-domiciled employees and wants to hire two overseas engineers — one based in India and one already in the UK on a Skilled Worker visa with a different employer.

**Week 1 — SMS Setup:**
Miriam's Level 1 User receives SMS credentials within three working days of approval. They log in, verify the company's details, and confirm the initial CoS allocation: 5 Undefined CoS and the ability to request Defined CoS as needed.

**Week 2 — Defined CoS for Priya (India-based):**
Miriam requests a Defined CoS for Priya via the SMS. She specifies the SOC code for a software developer role, an annual salary of £48,000 (illustrative figure, above the going rate threshold at the time of writing), and the anticipated start date. UKVI approves the request within one working day. Priya receives the CoS reference number and applies for a Skilled Worker visa at the British consulate in Mumbai. Processing takes approximately three weeks.

**Week 4 — Undefined CoS for Daniel (UK-based):**
Daniel, currently sponsored by a competitor, agrees to join Miriam's company. Miriam assigns an Undefined CoS from the existing allocation. Daniel applies to switch his Skilled Worker visa to his new employer. His visa is transferred and he starts within five weeks of the CoS assignment.

**Ongoing — Compliance:**
Miriam's HR manager sets calendar reminders for Daniel's and Priya's visa expiry dates, ensures right-to-work checks are filed, and tracks both workers' attendance against the SMS reporting requirements. Priya's visa expires in three years; the company will need to assign a new CoS and support Priya's visa renewal before that date.

**Total cost of sponsorship in Year 1 (illustrative):** The Immigration Skills Charge alone costs £1,000 per year per worker for medium or large sponsors (£364 for small sponsors and charities — figures illustrative based on 2026 rates, always verify current charges with UKVI). Add visa application fees, legal support, and premium processing if used.

## 5 Common Mistakes After Sponsor Licence Approval — and How to Avoid Them

1. **Treating the licence as a one-time task rather than an ongoing programme**
   *Mistake:* HR files the approval letter and moves on, with no system for compliance tracking.
   *Solution:* Immediately build a compliance calendar. Map every sponsored worker's visa expiry date, set 90-day, 60-day, and 30-day alerts, and assign a named individual responsible for SMS reporting.

2. **Assigning a CoS for a role that does not meet the salary or skill threshold**
   *Mistake:* Employers sometimes assign a CoS assuming the role qualifies, only for the worker's visa to be refused because the salary or SOC code is incorrect.
   *Solution:* Before assigning any CoS, verify the exact SOC code, the current going rate for that code, and the general salary threshold. Consult an immigration adviser if uncertain. Our article on [Why Sponsor Licence Applications Get Refused (2026)](/why-sponsor-licence-applications-get-refused/) covers role-eligibility errors in detail.

3. **Failing to report changes within the 10-working-day window**
   *Mistake:* A sponsored worker resigns and the HR team forgets to log the event in the SMS until three weeks later.
   *Solution:* Create an internal protocol — ideally written into your HR handbook — that any change involving a sponsored worker triggers an immediate SMS review. Do not wait for the payroll cycle.

4. **Naming a Key Personnel member who then leaves the business**
   *Mistake:* The Authorising Officer listed on the licence leaves, and no one updates the SMS. UKVI cannot make contact during a compliance check.
   *Solution:* Build Key Personnel succession planning into your HR processes. When a Key Personnel member gives notice, updating the SMS is a day-one task.

5. **Not requesting additional CoS allocations early enough**
   *Mistake:* A fast-growing business uses up its Undefined CoS allocation mid-year and cannot sponsor a business-critical hire for weeks while waiting for UKVI to process the increase request.
   *Solution:* Monitor your remaining allocation quarterly. When you fall below 20% of your annual allocation, submit an increase request — do not wait until you have zero left.

## Strategic Uses of a Sponsor Licence for Foreign-Owned UK Businesses

For foreign nationals managing UK entities, a sponsor licence is often a strategic tool, not merely an HR administrative mechanism.

**Global talent pipelines.** If your parent company is headquartered in Singapore, Germany, or Brazil, a UK sponsor licence allows you to rotate senior employees or specialists into your London office via the Global Business Mobility route without those individuals needing to compete in the open market.

**Retention of existing international hires.** If a valued employee's visa is expiring, the licence means you can assign a new CoS and support their extension without them having to find a new employer sponsor.

**Competitive hiring advantage.** In talent markets where the best candidates are globally mobile, being able to say "we are a licensed sponsor and can support your visa" is a meaningful differentiator over non-licenced competitors.

## What Your Licence Does Not Let You Do

It is equally important to understand the boundaries:

- **You cannot sponsor a worker for a role below the eligible skill or salary threshold**, regardless of how important the role is to your business.
- **You cannot transfer the licence** to another company entity, even a subsidiary or parent company. Each legal entity needs its own licence.
- **You cannot assign a CoS and then change the role significantly** without reporting the change or assigning a new CoS.
- **You cannot sponsor workers on routes not covered by your licence type** without applying to add those routes.
- **You cannot allow the licence to lapse** and continue assigning CoS — any CoS assigned on an expired licence is invalid.

## A Note on Compliance Visits and What to Expect

UKVI has the right to visit your premises — announced or unannounced — to verify that you are meeting your sponsor duties. During a visit, inspectors typically want to see:

- Evidence of right-to-work checks for all employees (not just sponsored workers)
- Records showing you are tracking sponsored workers' attendance and leave
- Payroll evidence that sponsored workers are being paid the salary stated on their CoS
- Evidence that Key Personnel are contactable and aware of their responsibilities
- Copies of sponsored workers' passports and visa documents

Being unprepared for a compliance visit is one of the fastest ways to lose a licence. The HR team — not just a director — needs to know where every document is, understand what each sponsored worker's status is, and be able to answer basic questions confidently.

## Final Thoughts

A sponsor licence, once approved, is one of the most commercially powerful tools a UK employer can hold. It opens direct access to the global talent market, enables intra-company transfers across borders, and signals to candidates that your business is set up to support international careers.

But it demands active, disciplined management. The businesses that use their licences most effectively are those that treat compliance not as a legal burden, but as a core HR process — one that is documented, audited internally, and owned by a named individual with the authority and knowledge to act quickly when circumstances change.

The licence is yours. What you build with it is up to you.
